Preface
The field of Materials Science and Engineering plays a critical role in ensuring the safety,
quality, and long-term sustainability of civil infrastructure. As a student of the Department of
Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Moratuwa, I had the opportunity to
undergo a six-month industrial training program at Consulting Engineering and Contractors
(Pvt) Ltd, under the supervision of the Sri Lanka Road Development Authority (RDA). This
internship allowed me to bridge the gap between academic learning and real-world engineering
practice within the context of a nationally significant rural road construction project.
The internship was carried out over a 24-week period and was structured into four progressive
stages, each designed to expose me to the technical, managerial, and practical aspects of road
development. I began with core field and laboratory investigations—such as Dynamic Cone
Penetration (DCP) tests, Proctor compaction, and sieve analysis—which laid the foundation for
understanding soil behaviour and subgrade performance. The second stage deepened my
knowledge in material sourcing, borrow pit evaluation, and laboratory testing on aggregates,
leading to a broader appreciation for quality control in road construction materials.
Subsequent stages focused on more advanced technical work, including pavement design,
compaction verification using field density tests, and participation in concrete works involving
mix design, slump testing, and cube casting. In the final phase, I was involved in bitumen
testing, asphalt mix validation, and on-site asphalt laying trials. A special highlight was
conducting a research study focused on the feasibility of reducing asphalt thickness as a cost-
saving and performance-optimized measure, a task that connected both technical analysis and
practical field evaluation.
This report captures the full scope of my internship experience—documenting technical
responsibilities, professional interactions, and personal development. The training enhanced
my practical understanding of materials testing, construction workflows, and site-level
decision-making, while also sharpening my problem-solving, documentation, and teamwork
skills.

1. Description of the Organization
Consulting Engineering and Contractors (Pvt) Ltd, commonly known as CEC, is a leading
construction firm in Sri Lanka specializing in infrastructure development, particularly in road
construction. The company was established in 1997 by Eng. Ashoka Randeni, and over the
years, it has built a strong reputation for delivering high-quality, cost-effective, and sustainable
engineering solutions. CEC is categorized under C1 by ICTAD (Institute for Construction
Training and Development), which signifies the highest level of registration for construction
contractors in Sri Lanka. The company is also certified under ISO 9001 standards, highlighting
its commitment to quality management practices.
CEC has played a major role in national infrastructure projects, particularly those funded by
international organizations such as the World Bank and Asian Development Bank. Its core
services include road and highway construction, drainage and bridge works, earthworks, and
asphalt production and paving. With a team of experienced engineers, technicians, and skilled
workers, CEC emphasizes professionalism, adherence to engineering standards, and innovation
in project delivery.

CEC’s vision is to be a nationally recognized leader in the construction sector, delivering
infrastructure that supports economic growth and community well-being. Its mission is to
provide reliable, efficient, and environmentally sustainable construction services that meet or

1.2.Organizational Structure
The company operates under a structured hierarchy to ensure smooth communication and
accountability. At the top of the structure is the Managing Director, followed by Project
Managers, Engineers, Quantity Surveyors, Material Engineers, Site Supervisors, Technicians,
and administrative staff.

2.2.1. Soil Investigations and Testing
The foundation of any road construction project lies in the quality of the soil. I actively
participated in subgrade evaluations and soil testing, particularly:
• Dynamic Cone Penetration (DCP) tests for estimating in-situ CBR values
• Proctor Compaction Test to determine OMC and MDD
• Sieve Analysis and Plasticity Index (PI) tests to classify soils (AASHTO and USCS)
• California Bearing Ratio (CBR) test in both soaked and unsoaked conditions
I learned to prepare soil samples, conduct field sampling procedures, and assist in the
preparation of laboratory reports under the guidance of the lab technician. These activities
helped me understand the correlation between soil properties and pavement performance.

3. Conclusion
3.1.Reflection on Internship Experience
The internship at Consulting Engineering and Contractors (Pvt) Ltd (CEC) offered me a
transformative opportunity to apply my academic knowledge to real-world engineering
practices. Over the 24-week training period, I was exposed to a wide spectrum of technical,
managerial, and personal development experiences. From soil testing and asphalt design to
report generation and stakeholder communication, each activity broadened my understanding
of how a construction project functions from concept to execution.
Working in both field and laboratory settings gave me firsthand experience in quality control
procedures and the importance of adhering to standards such as ICTAD and RDA guidelines. I
developed a clearer perspective on how engineering decisions impact safety, efficiency, and
cost. Moreover, witnessing the day-to-day interactions among engineers, technicians, and
workers enhanced my soft skills, especially in communication and teamwork.
3.2.Areas for Improvement in the Internship Process
While the internship was largely successful, there are areas that could be improved to enhance
the learning outcomes for future trainees:

=== PAGE 27 ===
• Structured Orientation: A more formalized orientation schedule would help trainees
adapt more quickly. Though I received adequate initial guidance, a structured training
manual or daily plan would make the transition smoother.
• Mentorship Assignment: Assigning each intern a dedicated mentor (e.g., Site Engineer
or Material Engineer) could streamline knowledge transfer and help resolve doubts
more efficiently.
• Rotational Exposure: Enabling interns to rotate through different departments such as
surveying, estimation, and procurement would offer a more holistic understanding of
the project cycle.
• Access to Digital Tools: Increased exposure to construction management software such
as AutoCAD Civil 3D, Primavera, or project management tools would provide valuable
industry skills.
3.3.Self-Assessment and Growth
Personally, I experienced significant growth during this internship. I became more confident in
performing technical tests, managing documentation, and interacting with professionals at
different levels. Initially hesitant in decision-making, I gradually took more responsibility and
contributed to quality assessments and troubleshooting.
The hands-on training refined my practical engineering judgment and strengthened my interest
in infrastructure development. I now have a clearer career direction and am better prepared to
handle the responsibilities of a graduate engineer in the construction sector.
This internship has not only enriched my academic learning but has also shaped my professional
mindset, emphasizing discipline, safety, collaboration, and continuous learning. I am grateful
for this experience and look forward to applying these lessons in my future career.
